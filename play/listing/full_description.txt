Geo turns your phone into a pocket altimeter, peak finder, and field-guide overlay — all running locally, with no account, no ads, and no tracking.

Open the Nature tab and the camera names the mountains in front of you. Open the Map tab and Geo pins your position alongside the known peaks around you. Open the Info tab for your altitude, the air pressure around you, today's sun times, and what a geomagnetic storm is doing to your compass.

LIVE ALTITUDE

Geo shows the device barometer's altitude and the GPS-derived altitude side by side. The barometer responds faster to a short climb; GPS is the more reliable long-term reference. Seeing both — and the gap between them — tells you more than either alone.

NAME THE PEAKS YOU SEE

Hold the camera up on the Nature tab and every mountain gets a label — name and height — on a thin line rising from the real summit. Only peaks that clear your horizon are labelled, and a minimum-altitude slider hides the small hills so you keep just the big mountains. Tap a peak for its distance, bearing and directions. Drag sideways to nudge the overlay onto the real ridge when the compass is a few degrees off. The shutter captures the view with the labels drawn on, ready to share. Download an area first, from the Info tab, and it all keeps working with no signal, out to ~80 km. Needs a device that supports ARCore.

MAGNETIC CONDITIONS

A card on the Info tab reads the planetary K index from NOAA's Space Weather Prediction Center — measured by ground observatories, not by your phone — and works out the local consequences on the device: the storm level on the G scale, how far a storm can push your compass beyond its normal error, whether GPS may degrade where you are, and whether the auroral oval reaches you tonight, with the darkness window and the bearing to look along. Aurora alerts are opt-in and off by default. The request to NOAA is a fixed URL with no parameters — none of it tells anyone where you are.

MAP

A Google Maps view with your position and every known peak in your area pinned. Tap a pin for its elevation and a quick info card.

HISTORY & WIDGET

The barometer is sampled in the background and kept in a local database, so the Stat tab plots pressure trends over hours and days — useful for spotting weather on the way.

A home-screen widget shows your current pressure, altitude, position and speed without opening the app.

PRIVACY

Geo doesn't collect personal information, doesn't create accounts, and uses no analytics, advertising, or trackers. Your pressure history, trips and summits stay on the device. What goes out goes on your behalf, to render something you asked for: Google's Maps SDK draws the map; OpenStreetMap's free Overpass API and Open-Meteo return peak names, ground elevation and the reference pressure that calibrates the altimeter, on approximate coordinates; and NOAA's planetary K index arrives from a fixed URL with no parameters of any kind — byte-identical for every user, carrying nothing about you. No data goes to a server we run; we have no servers.

The full privacy policy is at https://nettrash.me/play/geo/privacy.html.

PERMISSIONS

• Location (precise) — to show your position and find nearby peaks.
• Camera — for the Nature view; frames are processed on-device by ARCore.
• High-rate sensors — to read the device's barometer and orientation.
• Notifications — for the storm warning, and aurora alerts if you switch them on.

Geo does not request permission to read your contacts, calendar, photos, microphone, phone state, or installed-app list.
